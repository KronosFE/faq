Digital twin / 3D model

The interactive 3D model of the machine — what it is, what's open, and what's protected. Canon-locked, no financials.

What is the Kronos digital twin?
An interactive 3D model of the machine where each component is clickable and linked to the physics, the gate and the code behind it. It's a way to explore the design in three dimensions — see the digital twin.
Is it public?
A public version is openly viewable. The full, detailed model with CAD is behind a login, because the detailed engineering is protected intellectual property. The public view shows the design and its physics without exposing proprietary CAD.
What can I do with it?
Click a component — a magnet, the blanket, the divertor — and jump to the physics that governs it, the code that computed it and the gate it closes. It's the visual companion to the physics map and the V&V matrix.
Is the model consistent with the frozen design?
Yes. The twin is canon-locked and reconciled to the ratified 2026-08-27 freeze, so its numbers (Q 3.076 and the rest) match the register and the papers — no drift between the picture and the record.
How does it relate to the new 3D flux surfaces?
The interactive 3D flux surfaces are a separate, standalone visualization of the plasma shape at δ=−0.30 — a companion to the twin, not a replacement. The twin shows the machine; the flux view shows the plasma inside it.
Why is some of it behind a login?
The full CAD and detailed engineering are the parts most valuable to protect as IP. Keeping them behind a login lets us share the design openly while safeguarding the proprietary detail.
Is it a live model of an operating machine?
No. It's a design and simulation model of a conceptual machine — not telemetry from an operating plant. We're explicit about the stage everywhere.
