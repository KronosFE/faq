The fusion neutron economy

The idea at the heart of the materials-first strategy — that the neutron is the product. Canon-locked, no financials.

What is the "neutron economy"?
The idea that a fusion machine's neutrons are a product in themselves — breeding tritium, transmuting targets into medical and industrial isotopes, and enabling neutron services — not merely a step on the way to electricity. Sell what the machine makes first.
Why does it matter?
It reframes fusion's value. Value from neutrons — tritium first — can arrive before net-electricity breakeven, turning one distant milestone into several nearer ones. That's the whole materials-first wedge; see the landscape.
What does a single neutron "do" economically?
Three things, depending on where it goes: breed tritium in the blanket (the fuel fusion needs); transmute a target into a medical or industrial isotope; or be used for neutron services such as materials testing and activation analysis. Same neutron, several uses.
How is this different from the usual fusion story?
The usual story makes electricity the only product, at a distant breakeven. The neutron economy recognizes several nearer products from the same neutrons — with tritium as the bankable base, and isotopes and helium-3 as additional value (He-3 strategic, not booked as revenue). See isotopes.
Is it just theory?
It's the strategic thesis, grounded in the breeder as a platform — a computed tritium breeding ratio up to 1.42 and a full neutronics basis (OpenMC), published and checkable in the register.
Where can I read the full case?
The neutron-economy thesis runs through the 44-paper series, and the physics behind the neutrons is in OpenMC and the De-Risking Register.
Does this replace the electricity goal?
No — it sequences it. Electricity remains the long-term prize (the burner and the fleet); the neutron economy is how value and de-risking arrive on the way there, rather than only at the end.
