Safety & environment

The safety questions people ask about fusion — answered plainly and grounded in the computed neutronics. Canon-locked, no financials.

Can a fusion plant melt down?
No. Fusion has no chain reaction and no critical mass. The reaction has to be actively sustained every moment, so any loss of heating, field or fuel makes the plasma cool and simply extinguish. Its failure mode is "off." See Environment & Safety.
What radioactive waste does fusion make?
Fast neutrons activate the surrounding structure, and tritium is a radioactive fuel — but there are no long-lived fission products like a fission reactor. Because low-activation materials are chosen up front (screened by materials modeling, checked against the computed activation inventory), the structure decays to recyclable levels on a human timescale — no deep-geologic high-level-waste repository. Computed with ALARA · OpenMC.
Is tritium dangerous?
Tritium is radioactive — a low-energy beta-emitter — and is the main radiological inventory. It's handled in a closed fuel cycle, with inventory and permeation tracked by TMAP8. The breeder is designed to make its own, reducing reliance on external supply.
Can fusion be used for weapons?
The power cycle produces and requires no fissile material, so there is no weapons-material pathway through the plant.
What about an accident — how bad could it get?
The worst-case release is bounded and local — the physics does not support a large off-site event, because there is no large stored energy or runaway path. The fuel in the chamber at any instant is measured in milligrams.
What is the environmental footprint?
No combustion, so no operational CO₂ or air pollutants. Fuel is effectively unlimited — deuterium from water, lithium for breeding. The compact spherical-tokamak footprint gives high power density for the site area.
Is any of this an operating-plant safety case?
No — it's a computed safety and environmental assessment of a conceptual design, canon-locked to the 2026-08-27 freeze, not the safety analysis report of a licensed plant. We're explicit about the stage on every page.
