Defense & national security

How the low-neutron burner applies to installations and resilience — at a high level. Canon-locked, no financials.

What is the defense application of Kronos fusion?
Aegis is the burner in a defense housing: resilient, on-site power for fixed installations. It gives energy independence from a vulnerable grid and from fuel logistics — the same burner core that powers datacenters as MetroVolt.
Is Aegis a naval reactor?
No. Aegis is for fixed defense installations, not ships. Two housings, one burner core: Aegis for installations, MetroVolt for datacenter power.
Why does the military value fusion power?
Energy resilience is a strategic advantage: on-site power that doesn't depend on a grid or on fuel convoys, with no combustion and no emissions. A compact, low-neutron machine can be sited near people and hardware.
What makes the burner suited to installations?
D-³He fuel gives a low neutron fraction (5.44%), which eases shielding and materials for a compact unit — see the burner FAQ. It closes on-model at engineering gain Q_E 1.318, with the plug to be demonstrated at WHAM-scale.
Could fusion be used for weapons?
No. The power cycle produces and requires no fissile material — there is no weapons-material pathway through the plant. See safety.
