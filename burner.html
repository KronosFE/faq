The burner — Aegis / MetroVolt

How the D-³He tandem-mirror burner works, and why it's one core in two housings. Canon-locked, no financials.

What is the Aegis / MetroVolt burner?
A D-³He tandem-mirror burner — one core in two housings: Aegis for fixed defense installations and MetroVolt for datacenter power. It closes on-model at engineering gain Q_E 1.318 with P(Q_E>1) = 1.0.
Why D-³He fuel?
D-³He fusion produces far fewer neutrons than the usual D-T reaction — a neutron fraction of just 5.44%. That makes it a low-neutron burner, easing shielding, activation and materials for a compact power unit sited near people or hardware.
What is a tandem mirror?
A linear magnetic "bottle" with strong end plugs that keep the plasma from escaping out the ends. Its signature challenge is the loss-cone (drift-cyclotron, or DCLC) instability; Kronos shows it's stabilized on-model with sloshing ions. See the mirror deep-dive.
What is the WHAM-scale demonstration?
The burner closes on-model — but a plug that works in simulation isn't the same as one shown in hardware. So Kronos ties the plug requirement to a named WHAM-scale experiment (targeted 2032). The physics is computed; the plug is to be demonstrated, not presumed.
Why two housings for one core?
The same burner core serves two markets with different packaging: Aegis as fixed defense installations, MetroVolt as datacenter power. Fuel follows purpose, not platform — one machine, two missions.
Is the burner net-positive?
It closes on-model at engineering gain Q_E 1.318 — more useful energy out than in, in the model, with certainty across the ensemble (P(Q_E>1)=1.0). The two-tier honest gate: closed on-model now, plug demonstrated by the WHAM-scale experiment.
