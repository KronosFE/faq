Isotopes — medical & industrial

What a fusion neutron platform makes on the way to electricity. Canon-locked, no financials.

What does Kronos mean by an "isotope platform"?
The Hyperion breeder's neutron flux breeds tritium — its bankable base product — and the same neutron source can produce medical and industrial isotopes. This is the materials-first wedge: sell what the machine makes on the way to electricity. See the wedge.
What is the bankable base product?
Tritium. It's the fuel fusion needs and the world is short of, and it's the base the whole strategy rests on. Medical/industrial isotopes and helium-3 are additional strategic value — not the base, and not the thing the case depends on.
What are the medical isotopes used for?
Diagnostic imaging and targeted radionuclide therapy. Global medical-isotope supply is fragile — it leans on a small number of aging research reactors — so a new, reliable neutron source is strategically useful. The specific production routes are part of Kronos's intellectual property.
What about industrial isotopes?
Industrial radioisotopes are used in sterilization, non-destructive testing and inspection, materials analysis and sensing — steady, real-world demand served by neutron sources.
How does helium-3 fit in?
Helium-3 is genuinely valuable — for neutron detection, cryogenics and national security — and is chronically scarce. But Kronos treats its value as strategic, not as booked revenue: it strengthens the platform's importance without being counted as income. The bankable line stays tritium.
Why does this de-risk the business?
Because value arrives with materials — tritium first — before, and independent of, net-electricity breakeven. The breeder is a platform measured in tritium and isotopes, which is a much nearer gate than the grid. See the breeder FAQ.
Is isotope production happening now?
No — it's a capability of the conceptual design. The machine is at design and simulation stage, not operating; we're explicit about that everywhere.
