MetroVolt — datacenter power

On-site, low-neutron fusion power for datacenters — the burner in a datacenter housing. Canon-locked, no financials.

What is MetroVolt?
MetroVolt is the Kronos burner in a datacenter housing — on-site, low-neutron fusion power co-located with compute. It's the same D-³He tandem-mirror burner core as Aegis, packaged for datacenter campuses instead of defense installations.
Why fusion for datacenters?
AI-era compute demand needs clean, 24/7 baseload that doesn't strain the grid. On-site fusion gives continuous power with no emissions and no fuel logistics, sited right at the load — power where the racks are.
Is MetroVolt the same machine as Aegis?
Yes — the same burner core in a different housing. Aegis serves fixed defense installations; MetroVolt serves datacenters. Fuel follows purpose, not platform: one machine, two missions. See the burner FAQ.
Why does low-neutron matter for a datacenter?
D-³He fuel gives a low neutron fraction (5.44%), which eases shielding, activation and materials — so a compact unit can be sited near hardware and people rather than behind a large exclusion zone.
Is it net-positive power?
It closes on-model at engineering gain Q_E 1.318 with certainty across the ensemble (P(Q_E>1)=1.0). The two-tier honest gate: closed on-model now, with its plug requirement demonstrated by a named WHAM-scale experiment — shown, not assumed.
When could datacenters actually use it?
On the current plan, the burner is demonstrated in 2032 and fleet build begins from 2036 — dates are targets tied to gates. See the roadmap. Today it is a conceptual engineering program, not an operating unit.
